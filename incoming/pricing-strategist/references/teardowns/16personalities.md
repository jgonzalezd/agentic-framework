## Ladder arithmetic

**There are two rungs and one of them is free, so most per-unit checks do not apply.** What can be computed:

**The bundle claim, and it is checkable in one direction only.** "Save 70% vs. buying individual types" against a $29 suite implies the à-la-carte equivalent would be about **$96.67** — derived, not observed. **The individual-type prices were not captured**, so the claim cannot be verified, only restated. Note the framing choice: **the saving is expressed against the company's own unbundled prices**, which is the cheapest possible anchor to construct and the hardest for a buyer to check.

**Guarantee arithmetic, and this is the important one.** The product is a digital artefact with **zero marginal cost** and a **30-day, no-questions-asked refund**. The exposure is therefore bounded by the refund rate alone, with no COGS to recover. **At $29 on zero marginal cost, the refund rate is a rounding error against the conversion lift** — which is precisely why 16Personalities can offer the most generous guarantee in the decision-help neighbourhood while `figma.md` writes "non-refundable" and six of seven consumer-subscription pages offer nothing at all.

**Price positioning against the neighbourhood's verified bands:**
- **$29 sits at the top of the self-serve artefact band ($9.95–$36)** established by four independent products (see `one-off-report-market.md`).
- It sits **just below the "$39/call" floor** for the cheapest single human answer (`mentorcruise.md`) — **near enough that the comparison flatters the report, far enough that nobody mistakes it for a call.**
- **The $36–$39 seam is where the buyer's comparison set flips from "a report" to "a person."** $29 is deliberately on the safe side of it.

**Free-tier arithmetic.** The free result costs the company inference and hosting on **1.56 billion tests** and returns **"337K+ Tests taken today"** of live top-of-funnel. **The giveaway is the acquisition channel**, and the paid artefact monetises the fraction who want more after being satisfied.

## Social proof, trust and objection handling

**Scale-as-credential at a magnitude no credential could match:**

> **"our free personality test has been taken more than a billion times"** · **"1.56B+ Total tests taken"** · **"197M+ Tests taken in United States"** · **"337K+ Tests taken today"** · **"91.2% Accuracy rating"** · available **"in 49 languages"**

**Two details worth isolating.**

**"337K+ Tests taken today" is a live-activity counter** — it proves the thing is alive *right now*, which a cumulative total cannot. A billion is impressive and inert; 337,000 today is evidence the product still works.

**"91.2% Accuracy rating" is a self-reported, precisely-decimalised claim that functions as a science signal without being one.** The decimal is doing the persuading. Compare `talkspace.md`'s "4,999,000 therapy sessions" — the same technique, applied to a count rather than to a quality claim.

**The guarantee does the real closing work.** **"no questions asked"** removes the last objection to a $29 impulse purchase **whose value cannot be assessed before delivery** — which is the defining property of an insight artefact.

**And the quality claim is borrowed, not asserted.** **"freakishly accurate"** appears **in quotation marks** — it is a user's word, not the company's, **which is why it is believable.** A company describing its own output as freakishly accurate would be discounted instantly.

## What transfers, and what doesn't

**Scale-driven:** "1.56B+ Total tests taken" and "49 languages" are unavailable to anyone else, and the free-forever core is affordable at that scale because the marginal cost of a test is near zero and the traffic is organic.

**Genuinely universal, and this is the single most transferable page in the decision-help neighbourhood for a self-serve artefact product:**

1. **Put the paywall after the promised value, not before it.** Give away the thing that makes the user feel seen; sell the thing they want *because* they feel seen. This is the endowment effect used honestly — the user has already received something, so the purchase is an extension rather than a gamble.
2. **Lead with the time cost, before money.** "Only 10 minutes" is the first price the buyer accepts, and it is the cheaper one.
3. **Offer an unconditional, no-questions-asked refund on any sub-$50 digital good whose value cannot be judged before purchase.** Zero marginal cost means the refund rate is a rounding error and the conversion lift is not. **This is the standard mechanism for the category and almost nobody in the wider corpus uses it.**
4. **Put the quality claim in the customer's mouth and the quotation marks around it.** "Freakishly accurate" works because 16Personalities did not say it.
5. **Say "one-time purchase" explicitly if you are one.** It is a differentiator in a market the buyer has learned to distrust.
6. **Publish a live-activity counter alongside any cumulative total.** "337K+ today" proves the product is alive; a billion proves only that it once was.
7. **State the price band honestly against your neighbours.** $29 for a no-human structured artefact is the top of a tight, four-product verified band — and the ceiling is real: **no researched product in this shape prices above $36.**
