## Ladder arithmetic

**The cleanest and most honest annual framing in the decision-help neighbourhood, and every figure reconciles.**

- $12.99 × 12 = **$155.88**, against the struck-through anchor of **"$155.99/yr"** — a $0.11 rounding, so **the anchor is the buyer's own monthly price annualised, not an invented list price.**
- $107.99 ÷ $155.99 = 0.6923 → **30.77% off**. **"SAVE 30%" is real rather than inflated.**
- $107.99 ÷ 12 = **$8.999** → **"$8.99/mo"** ✓.

**Contrast this with the corpus norm.** Most strikethrough anchors are constructed — a list price nobody pays, or an à-la-carte total that is never published (`16personalities.md`'s "Save 70% vs. buying individual types"). **Rosebud's anchor is the actual alternative the buyer would otherwise choose**, which makes the saving verifiable in one multiplication.

**Monthly-to-annual-equivalent ratio: 1.445×** ($12.99 vs $8.99). That is **well below the 2.2×–2.9× band** the consumer-subscription cohort clusters in (see `calm.md`, `headspace.md`, `blinkist.md`). **Rosebud does not punish monthly**; it simply discounts annual. The two strategies produce very different relationships with a buyer whose need may end.

**Price positioning against the neighbourhood's verified bands:**
- **$8.99–$12.99/month sits squarely in the AI-advice cluster of $8.99–$19.99** (with `abby.md` at $19.99 and Talkspace's "Tee AI Plan" at $19.99).
- Annualised, **$107.99/yr is roughly 3× the ceiling of the one-off artefact band** ($9.95–$36, `one-off-report-market.md`).
- Against the therapy alternative the copy implies: a single market-rate session is **$100–$200** (Psychology Today), so **a full year of Rosebud costs about what one therapy session costs.** The page never states this arithmetic — **"minutes, not months"** makes the same point on the time axis instead, and **because it never mentions a price, it cannot be argued with on price.**

## Social proof, trust and objection handling

> **⚠ The page's own social proof is internally inconsistent and must be handled carefully.** Separate extraction passes over the same page surfaced **"150K+ users," "35,311 reviews," and "735,311 ratings"** — conflicting with the 5K/100K figures **by two orders of magnitude.** **Only "4.9 stars" and "Trusted by 100K+ users" should be treated as reportable, and both are single-source.**

What is reportable:

- **"Trusted by 100K+ users"** · 4.9 stars across 5K+ reviews
- **Endorsements attributed by profession rather than by name** — a Positive Psychologist, an Executive Coach, a Family Therapist, an IFS Therapist. **Credential-by-profession**: the title does the work a name would, and no individual is exposed.
- **Institutional logo bar:** "Trusted by employees at Google, Microsoft, Meta, Y Combinator, UC Berkeley, MIT, NYU." Note the careful construction — **"employees at"**, not "used by", which claims nothing about the institutions themselves.
- **Self-reported efficacy at 7 days, n=700:** anger 63% improved, grief 52%, ADHD 40%.

**Privacy, and the hedges are structural rather than careless.** **"Your data is encrypted in transit and at rest"** is explicitly **not** end-to-end — Rosebud must be able to read entries in order to run AI over them. **"HIPAA aligned"** is a marketing formulation, not "compliant" and not certified.

**This is the defining constraint of the AI-journalling category and the copy shows it. AI-over-your-text and end-to-end encryption are structurally incompatible.** Compare `journaling-cohort.md`, where Day One — which runs no AI over entries — can write **"Even if we wanted to read what's in your journal (we don't), we couldn't."** Rosebud can only claim it encrypts what it *can* read.

## What they do that most pages don't

**They anchor against therapy on the time axis rather than the price axis.** **"Feel better in minutes, not months."** is the most efficient therapy anchor found in the neighbourhood. **It never mentions a price, so it cannot be argued with on price** — and it sidesteps the regulatory hazard of claiming to substitute for care.

**They let testimonials make the substitution claim they cannot make themselves.** The page positions the product as "a companion rather than replacement" in its own voice — **"not a substitute for a human connection"** — while user quotes carry the argument:

> **"ONLY 12.99 a month, way cheaper than a therapist"**
> **"Much cheaper than private talk therapy"**
> **"better than the therapists that I have paid for"**
> **"so much better than in person therapy with a human"**
> **"I prefer this over seeing my psychiatrist/therapist"**

**This is the standard structure for a regulated-adjacent claim: first-party hedges, third-party asserts.** It is also the reason the price comparison never appears in Rosebud's own voice anywhere on the page.

**Their annual saving is arithmetically true.** In a corpus full of constructed anchors, "SAVE 30%" against the buyer's own annualised monthly price is unusual and checkable.

## What transfers, and what doesn't

**Category-driven:** the professional endorsements and the science page are responses to operating adjacent to a regulated field. A product outside that adjacency needs neither.

**Genuinely universal:**
1. **Anchor on time, not price, when the alternative is expensive and slow.** "Minutes, not months" makes the comparison without inviting a price argument, and without making a claim a regulator would read.
2. **Let your customers say what you cannot.** First-party hedges, third-party asserts. If a substitution claim is legally or ethically risky in your voice, it is still available in theirs — and more credible there.
3. **Anchor your saving to the buyer's real alternative, and make the arithmetic check.** "$107.99/yr, regularly $155.99/yr, SAVE 30%" reconciles in one multiplication because $155.99 *is* twelve months of the monthly plan. **A constructed anchor invites the buyer to check; a real one survives being checked.**
4. **Do not punish monthly if your buyer's need might end.** A 1.445× ratio says "annual is better"; a 2.9× ratio says "monthly is a mistake". Choose deliberately.
5. **Know what your architecture lets you claim about privacy, and do not overreach.** Encryption at rest is not end-to-end, and "aligned" is not "compliant". **A user who notices the gap stops believing the rest of the page** — and in this category trust is loss-averse (see `betterhelp.md`: 83% remained confident *after* a $7.8m settlement; the asymmetry cuts both ways).
6. **Audit your own social-proof numbers before shipping them.** Rosebud's page carries figures that differ from each other by two orders of magnitude. A buyer who spots that has been handed a reason to disbelieve the rest.
