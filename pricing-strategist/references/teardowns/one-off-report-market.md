## Structure, top to bottom

## Ladder arithmetic

**The verified band, and it is the most reliable number in the decision-help neighbourhood:**

> **A one-off, self-serve, no-human personal-insight deliverable bears $9.95–$36.** Four independent products cluster tightly — UnderstandMyself **$9.95**, Enneagram RHETI **$20**, 16Personalities **$29**, Enneagram bundle **$36**. **No researched product in this shape prices above $36.**

**The Enneagram bundle discount, checked.** "$40 $36 both tests" — the strikethrough is $20 + $20 = $40, and the bundle is $36, a **10.0% discount, or $4.00.** Per test that is **$18.00 versus $20.00.** A modest discount, applied to a $40 digital good, **and the strikethrough is honest arithmetic on the company's own published single-test price** — unlike a bundle claim anchored to unpublished à-la-carte prices (compare `16personalities.md`'s unverifiable "Save 70% vs. buying individual types").

**Price per minute of effort asked** — a useful cross-product check, because these products differ more in time demanded than in price:

| Product | Price | Effort | **$/minute of effort** |
|---|---|---|---|
| UnderstandMyself | $9.95 | 10–20 min | **$0.50–$1.00** |
| Enneagram RHETI | $20.00 | ~40 min | **$0.50** |
| 16Personalities Premium | $29.00 | ~10 min | **$2.90** |

**The two Enneagram/Big-Five products converge at roughly $0.50 per minute of buyer effort**, while 16Personalities charges nearly 6× that per minute — **because it charges *after* delivering a free result, so its $29 buys additional depth rather than the initial experience.** The comparison shows the two available positions: **charge little for a long effort you have not yet justified, or charge more for depth after you have already proved yourself.**

**Price per 1,000 words of deliverable**, where specified. Enneagram RHETI supplies "Expanded Type Descriptions (of over 2,500 words each) for your top three scores" = **at least 7,500 words** for $20, i.e. **$2.67 per 1,000 words at most.** No other product in this cohort specifies its output length, which is exactly why the Enneagram Institute can defend a $20 price on a report nobody has seen.

**Does any add-on invert the ladder?** No add-ons exist in any of the three.

**Does a lower tier plus add-ons reach a higher tier?** The only stacking is Enneagram's bundle, and it is priced **below** the sum of its parts ($36 < $40), which is the correct direction — **buying the two tests separately is strictly worse, so the bundle cannot be arbitraged.**

**Expiry arithmetic.** "Test codes expire one year from date of purchase" is **gentle urgency without a manufactured countdown** — a real constraint with a real reason (single-use codes must be redeemed), stated once. Compare `blinkist.md`'s permanently-running "20% discount reserved for🔥 10:00".

**Refund arithmetic:** **none available for any of the three.** Given zero marginal cost, this is a missed lever — `16personalities.md` demonstrates the alternative at the same price point.

## Social proof, trust and objection handling

**Trust in this cohort is built by specification rather than by proof, and that is the transferable insight.** None of the three publishes user counts, testimonials, ratings or logos. What they publish instead:

1. **Science-model naming** — **"based on the Big Five Aspects Scale, the scientific model that describes your personality…"** (UnderstandMyself). A named intellectual lineage substituting for a credential.
2. **Deliverable specification by word count** — **"over 2,500 words each"** (Enneagram).
3. **Format concreteness** — **"available in a PDF format"** (Enneagram).
4. **Effort disclosure up front** — "144 paired statements... about 40 minutes" (Enneagram), "10 to 20 minutes" (UnderstandMyself), "approximately 10-15 minutes" (Truity).
5. **Unit honesty** — "one, single-use test code" states exactly what is being bought and what it is not.

**Specifying the artefact — its format and its length — is how a report justifies a price when no human is involved.** "Detailed insights" is a claim; "over 2,500 words each, available in a PDF format" is a specification, and only one of them can be checked.

## What transfers, and what doesn't

**Brand-driven, and this is the one thing not to copy blindly:** the Enneagram Institute's pay-before-effort funnel works because "Enneagram Institute" means something to its buyer. **A product nobody has heard of cannot ask for $20 before a 40-minute commitment.** For an unknown product the sequence must be reversed — deliver first, charge second (`16personalities.md`) — or the price must fall to UnderstandMyself's near-impulse **$9.95**.

**Genuinely universal, and this group is the closest unit-economics analogue in the corpus for any no-human digital deliverable:**

1. **The band is $9.95–$36 and the ceiling is real.** Four independent products, no exceptions above $36. **Above roughly $36–$39 the buyer's comparison set flips from "a report" to "a person"** — and `mentorcruise.md`'s "$39/call" is what a person costs. **Do not price above $36 without adding a human.**
2. **Specify the deliverable physically.** Word count, format, structure, number of sections. This is how a price is justified when there is no credential to point at, and it is free.
3. **Name your intellectual lineage.** "Based on the Big Five Aspects Scale" performs the function of a credential without claiming one. Whatever your method draws on, name it and count it.
4. **Disclose the effort in minutes, up front.** The first cost the buyer accepts should be time, not money — and disclosing it honestly converts better than hiding it.
5. **Use a real constraint for urgency, not a manufactured one.** "Test codes expire one year from date of purchase" is a term, not a tactic.
6. **Add the guarantee this cohort is missing.** None of these three offers a refund; `16personalities.md` offers 30 days, no questions asked, at the top of the same band. **At zero marginal cost the refund rate is a rounding error and the conversion lift is not** — this is the cheapest unclaimed lever in the group.
