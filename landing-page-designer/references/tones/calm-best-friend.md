# Calm Best Friend

## 1. Identity
The voice of a close friend who's been where you are and isn't worried about you — warm, steady, zero pressure. It reassures before it persuades; the reader should exhale a little. (The Calm / Headspace register.) **Use for:** wellness, sleep, mental health, habit-building, health, parenting, anything the visitor arrives at feeling behind or anxious. **Avoid for:** pro tools, finance/data apps where authority converts better, or audiences who read warmth as fluff.

## 2. Voice attributes
- Contractions always; second person always.
- Acknowledges the hard part before offering the fix — never pretends it's easy.
- Invites, never commands: "you can start tonight," not "start now."
- Permission-giving: it's explicitly okay to be a beginner, to have quit before, to go slow.
- Questions used gently, to show recognition — not to corner the reader.
- No hype words at all; the tone earns trust by staying under-stated.

## 3. Rhythm rules
Short sentences, 5–14 words, with the occasional longer, softer one to slow the pace. Periods over exclamation points — exclamation marks are banned in this tone. Fragments allowed when they soothe ("No pressure. No streaks to lose."). One to two sentences per paragraph; white space is part of the voice.

## 4. Vocabulary
**Favor:** gentle, easy, okay, small, steady, at your own pace, one step, tonight, breathe, "that's normal", "you're not behind".
**Banned in this tone:** crush, dominate, hack, blast, insane, epic, grind, "no excuses", "level up", any body-shame framing ("get shredded", "beach body").

## 5. Example rewrites
- Hero headline: "Track your runs and build a running habit." → **"You can become a runner. One easy run at a time."**
- Subhead: "Trailhead helps beginners go from couch to consistent runner with guided plans." → **"Trailhead walks with you from your first five minutes to your first 5K. No pressure — just a plan that fits your life."**
- Feature blurb: "Guided audio runs tell you when to run and when to walk." → **"A friendly voice in your ear tells you when to run and when to walk. You never have to guess."**
- FAQ answer: "Trailhead starts easier than you expect and adjusts when you miss a day." → **"Quit a running app before? That's okay — most people have. Trailhead starts smaller than you'd think, and when you miss a day, it simply meets you where you are."**
- CTA: "Download Trailhead on the App Store." → **"Start your first easy run."**

## 6. Anti-patterns
- **Therapy-speak** — "hold space for your journey of becoming": clinical-wellness filler that sounds like a persona, not a friend. A friend says "it's okay to start small."
- **Saccharine overload** — "you beautiful, brave soul": warmth curdles into condescension. Keep the temperature at reassuring, not gushing.
- **Toothless copy** — so soft it never says what the app does. Calm is a register, not an excuse to drop the promise; the outcome must still be concrete.
- **Sneaky pressure** — "you owe it to yourself" is a guilt trip wearing a cardigan. This tone never leverages guilt.

## 7. Readability note
Naturally the easiest pack to keep at 8th grade — short warm words are its native material. The one tension: reassurance can balloon sentences with qualifiers ("maybe, if you feel like it, whenever you're ready…"). One softener per sentence, maximum; cut the rest.
