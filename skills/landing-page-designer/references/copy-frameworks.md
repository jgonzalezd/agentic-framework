# Copy Frameworks — techniques, the readability rule, and the natural-voice guard

Three layers, applied in order: a **framework** shapes each section's argument, the **eight principles** shape each line, and the **guards** (readability + recognition + natural voice) are pass/fail checks on every draft. The guards apply across ALL tone packs — no pack may override them.

## The eight principles (Leadpages)

1. **Lead with customer value** — every headline states what the visitor gets, not what the app has. "Fall asleep faster tonight," not "Advanced sleep-cycle algorithm."
2. **Emotional resonance** — name the pain in the visitor's own words before offering the fix. Copy that skips the feeling reads like a spec sheet.
3. **Numbers for credibility** — specific beats vague: "4.8★ · 12,400 reviews" beats "highly rated." Round numbers read as made up; real ones convert. Never invent them (see SKILL.md's proof rule).
4. **Plain language** — write like you talk. Short words, active voice, second person. Jargon is a tax the visitor won't pay.
5. **Cut fluff** — every sentence earns its place or goes. If a line can be deleted without the section losing meaning, delete it.
6. **Perfect grammar** — a typo on a landing page reads as a bug in the app. Proof every line.
7. **Address objections** — the FAQ section exists because unanswered doubt kills installs silently. Answer the top 3–5 honestly, including the awkward ones.
8. **Create urgency — only when true** — a real price change, a real seasonal reason, a real cohort start. Fabricated scarcity ("only 3 spots!") on an app-download page destroys the trust the rest of the page built.

## The readability rule (hard constraint)

**All page copy at or below 8th-grade reading level.** Evidence: U.S. PIAAC literacy data — only about 1 in 7 U.S. adults (~14%) reads proficiently above that level; AMA and CDC both recommend public-facing copy stay at or below 8th grade. A landing page is public-facing copy for strangers, not documentation for enthusiasts.

How to check each draft, in order of signal:
- Sentence length: average under ~15 words; no sentence over 25.
- Word choice: prefer 1–2 syllable words; a 3+ syllable word needs to earn its place ("consistent" earns it; "revolutionary" doesn't).
- Mental Flesch-Kincaid: if a sentence needs a second read, it fails.
- The out-loud test: read the section aloud; anywhere you stumble, the visitor stops scrolling.

This is a *ceiling on complexity*, not a floor on intelligence — Apple writes at this level. Simplicity is craft, not dumbing down.

## The recognition test (hard constraint)

**Would the target visitor say "that's me" — or "that's clever"? Clever = rewrite. Recognition is the hero's job; insight is content marketing's job.** A hook lands only when it mirrors something the visitor already feels, in words they'd use — which is why every hero/hook line must trace to a language-bank entry (see `customer-research.md` and SKILL.md's mined-not-invented rule).

Three corollaries, checked on every line:
- **The customer's words beat your words.** When a bank quote and your phrasing compete, the quote (or its lightest compression) wins.
- **Clarity over creativity.** A line the reader has to decode has already lost the tap.
- **Specificity beats abstraction.** "Groceries delivered in 1 hour" beats "Improve your workflow."

## Frameworks (pick per section, note the pick in `sections.md`)

- **AIDA** (Attention → Interest → Desire → Action) — the whole-page skeleton: hero grabs attention, hook/features build interest, proof/FAQ build desire, CTA is the action. Use it to check the scroll narrative has no dead zones.
- **PAS** (Problem → Agitate → Solve) — the hook section's native shape: name the pain, make it felt, present the app as the release. Agitate means "make it recognized," never "make it hurt more than it does."
- **StoryBrand** (visitor = hero, app = guide) — the framing rule for the whole page: the visitor is the protagonist; the app is the guide with a plan. Any sentence where the app is the hero ("We built the most advanced…") gets rewritten to put the visitor there ("You get…").

## The natural-voice guard (applies to every tone)

Marketing-speak is a register visitors have learned to skip. Two checks:

**1. Banned clichés — global list.** These words/phrases fail any draft, in any tone (tone packs add their own bans on top):

> seamless · unlock · unleash · elevate · effortless · supercharge · game-changer / game-changing · revolutionize / revolutionary · empower · next-level / take it to the next level · cutting-edge · world-class · best-in-class · like never before · in today's fast-paced world · look no further · journey (as filler — "your wellness journey") · delve · robust · leverage (as a verb) · solutions (for an app) · say goodbye to X, say hello to Y

If a banned word is genuinely the app's domain term (e.g. a lock app "unlocks" doors), it may appear in its literal sense only.

**2. The read-aloud test.** Read each section aloud as if telling a friend about the app. Any line you'd be embarrassed to say to a real person — too salesy, too breathless, too vague — gets rewritten in the words you'd actually use. This is the cheapest naturalness check that exists; run it on every section.
