# Minimal Clear

## 1. Identity
The voice of a product so sure of itself it barely raises its voice — sparse, literal, product-forward. No adjective it can't prove, no sentence that isn't load-bearing. The confidence is in what it leaves out. (The Apple register.) **Use for:** design-led apps, utilities, camera/photo tools, premium products, audiences fatigued by marketing. **Avoid for:** apps whose value needs explaining or whose category the visitor doesn't already understand — minimalism assumes the reader fills gaps, and an unknown category has no gaps to fill from.

## 2. Voice attributes
- States what the product does; never argues for it.
- Concrete nouns and numbers carry the meaning; adjectives appear only when provable ("free", "offline", "9-week").
- No direct address unless functionally needed — the product, not the reader, is the subject.
- Zero filler: no "so you can", no "designed to help you", no warm-up clauses.
- Silence as emphasis: the shortest section on the page is the one it's most confident about.
- Never explains a joke, a feature, or itself twice.

## 3. Rhythm rules
Sentences 3–12 words. Fragments are the native unit ("A 9-week plan. Audio cues for every run."). Periods only — no exclamation points, almost no commas, no em-dash chains. One to two lines per paragraph; the layout's white space is part of the copy.

## 4. Vocabulary
**Favor:** plain product nouns (plan, run, cue, week), numerals, verbs in simple present ("adjusts", "works offline").
**Banned in this tone:** beautiful/beautifully, simply, just (as minimizer), "we believe", "reimagined", "meet <App>", any intensifier (very, truly, incredibly) — the whole register collapses if one slips in.

## 5. Example rewrites
- Hero headline: "Track your runs and build a running habit." → **"Guided runs for beginners."**
- Subhead: "Trailhead helps beginners go from couch to consistent runner with guided plans." → **"A 9-week plan. Audio cues for every run. Adjusts when life gets in the way."**
- Feature blurb: "Guided audio runs tell you when to run and when to walk." → **"Audio cues tell you when to run and when to walk."**
- FAQ answer: "Trailhead starts easier than you expect and adjusts when you miss a day." → **"Stop for a week and the plan rebuilds around where you are now."**
- CTA: "Download Trailhead on the App Store." → **"Download Trailhead."**

## 6. Anti-patterns
- **Faux-Apple portentousness** — "Running. Reimagined.": mimics the rhythm of minimalism while saying nothing. Every fragment must carry a fact.
- **Minimal to the point of mute** — cutting so much the differentiator disappears. Sparse means every remaining word is load-bearing, not that few words remain.
- **Luxury drift** — swapping facts for atmosphere ("Crafted for the discerning runner"). This tone proves; it never poses.
- **Accidental coldness on human topics** — for a pain the reader feels personally, pure product-subject copy can read indifferent; one plain human line ("Most people quit week two. The plan knows that.") fixes it without breaking register.

## 7. Readability note
Trivially within the 8th-grade rule on sentence length; the risk is *compression* — a fragment that drops too much context ("Adaptive. Offline. Yours.") forces re-reading, which fails the rule in spirit. Test: each fragment must be understandable on first read by someone who skipped every other section.
