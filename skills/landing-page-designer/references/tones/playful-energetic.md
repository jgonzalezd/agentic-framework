# Playful Energetic

## 1. Identity
The voice of the funny friend who makes hard things feel light — quick, self-aware, momentum-driven. It wins the reader by making them smirk once and feel unjudged. (The Duolingo register.) **Use for:** games, learning apps, social apps, habit apps aimed at people who quit serious tools, younger or meme-literate audiences. **Avoid for:** health scares, money stress, grief/anxiety domains, or anywhere a joke lands as not taking the reader's problem seriously.

## 2. Voice attributes
- Humor from honesty: jokes name the true awkward thing ("we know you quit last time"), never random wackiness.
- Self-aware about being an app doing marketing; lightly punctures its own sales pitch.
- Momentum verbs and small dares — playful, but the challenge is always low-stakes.
- At most one joke per section; the promise is never the punchline.
- Exclamation points are a spice: maximum one per page, not per paragraph.
- Casual contractions, sentence-fragments, the occasional "Same." — talks like a group chat, spelled correctly.

## 3. Rhythm rules
Short and punchy: 3–12 words per sentence, fragments welcome. Rhythm does the humor — setup, beat, payoff. One to two sentences per paragraph. Em-dashes fine; ellipses and ALL-CAPS banned.

## 4. Vocabulary
**Favor:** tiny, ridiculously, actually, "let's be honest", "you know the drill", concrete funny specifics (couch, snooze button, "week three").
**Banned in this tone:** LOL/emoji-speak, "epic", "awesome-sauce", forced slang ("fam", "no cap"), anything mean about the reader — the joke is always *with* them, never at them.

## 5. Example rewrites
- Hero headline: "Track your runs and build a running habit." → **"Couch, meet 5K."**
- Subhead: "Trailhead helps beginners go from couch to consistent runner with guided plans." → **"Trailhead turns 'I should really run' into 'I ran today.' Guided runs, tiny wins, zero guilt."**
- Feature blurb: "Guided audio runs tell you when to run and when to walk." → **"A coach in your ear calls the shots — run, walk, breathe, repeat. You just show up."**
- FAQ answer: "Trailhead starts easier than you expect and adjusts when you miss a day." → **"Quit a running app before? Same. Trailhead starts comically easy and only levels up when you're actually ready. Miss a day and it shrugs, then re-plans."**
- CTA: "Download Trailhead on the App Store." → **"Lace up. Download Trailhead."**

## 6. Anti-patterns
- **Exclamation spam** — "Get fit! Have fun! Start now!": energy faked with punctuation instead of rhythm. One per page, if that.
- **Clown mode** — jokes so dense the reader can't find what the app does. Funny is the seasoning; the promise is the meal.
- **Cringe slang** — borrowed internet-speak ages in weeks and reads as a brand trying too hard. If a 40-year-old marketer typed it to sound young, cut it.
- **Punching down** — humor about the reader's failure ("couch potato much?") instead of alongside it ("we've all quit week two").

## 7. Readability note
Short punchy lines keep this pack comfortably under 8th grade. The risk is *referential* complexity, not word length — a joke that needs cultural context to land excludes readers just like a long word does. Every joke must also work read literally.
