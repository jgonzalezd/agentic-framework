# Audience Pack — [Audience Name]

> **How to use this template.** Copy to `references/audiences/<audience>.md` and fill every section — a pack with a hollow section produces hollow output in that section. The skill discovers audiences by listing this directory, so nothing else needs updating.
>
> **Before you start, answer the one question the pack exists to answer:** what is this audience actually buying, that the others aren't? If the answer is close to an existing pack's, you don't need a new pack — you need a note in that one. Press are buying a story their readers will click. Acquirers are buying a strategic position. Partners are buying reach into your users. Hiring candidates are buying a bet on their next three years. Each of those demands a different skeleton, not a different tone.
>
> **Use the same fictional example product across every pack** — *Sundial*, the night-shift sleep planner used in `customers.md` and `investors.md`. Shared example, different outputs is what makes packs comparable at a glance. Delete this block when done.

## What they're actually buying

One paragraph. Name the thing this audience wants that the product is a route to. Then draw out the hard consequence — what that means you must lead with, and what it means you must leave out.

If the audience's default awareness or posture matters (problem-aware, skeptical, time-boxed, reading in an inbox at speed), say so here. It changes the opening move.

## Section skeleton

The ordered sections this audience's description must contain. For each: a bolded name, then one or two sentences on the job it does and what makes it fail. Aim for 5–8 sections — fewer and the pack isn't giving structure, more and nothing gets drafted properly.

State whether sections may be cut for brevity and which ones are load-bearing.

## Register

Bulleted. Person (first/second/third), sentence length, readability target, formality, punctuation conventions, and the one stylistic trade this audience rewards (e.g. specific over clever, compressed over complete).

## Disqualifiers — instant loss

Bulleted. The things that make this audience stop reading or stop trusting. Be concrete: name the actual phrases and moves, not categories. Every pack inherits the global rule — invented numbers and invented social proof are disqualifying everywhere — but list the audience-specific ones here.

## Failure modes to watch for

3–5 named failure modes, each with a **bolded name**, a description of how it happens, and a test that catches it. These are what Phase 5 checks against, so make the tests actionable — something you can run against a draft, not a sentiment.

## Worked example

*Label it illustrative and state that the fictional product must never appear in a deliverable.*

Show the wrong version first, briefly, and name what's wrong with it. Then the right version at full length, in this pack's skeleton. Close with a note on what specifically changed and why it lands — the reasoning is what transfers to a new product; the words don't.
